I also have an idealistic idea for "participative democracy" that I think can
also be modelled by containers. It doesn't resolve the "budget allocation"
problem, its more about decision making. Perhaps it could be extended to budget
allocation.

So you have "politically active" citizens who spend time in "chat rooms"
debating government policy. You can also have "political parties". Voting is
delegation. Political parties can delegate their vote to specifical individual
"politically active" citizens for specific decisions. Every "regular citizen"
gets a vote on EVERY issue. But obviously they don't have time to consider every
political issue so they delegate their vote to specific "political parties" or
"politically active" citizen.

Obviously any "regular citizen" can choose to become a "politically active"
citizen if they are so inclined but it is not required. Any "politically active"
citizen can join a "political party".

So, suppose the government has three branches: Health, Education and Military.
And you have three major "political parties" A, B and C. The "lazy" thing to do
(if you are busy and don't want to spend time thinking about politics) is to
delegate your vote to party B for all three categories. Health, Education and
Military. Or you can delegate your vote to party A for health and party C for
both Education and Military.

Each category becomes more finally grained. For example: Health decomposes as
"Early Childhood Development", STEM, "art and creativity", "physical education
and health". You might be particularly concerned about "art and creativity", so
you delegate A to Education, and C to both Health and Military. But you "refine"
your vote to Education so that A gets your vote for "Early Childhood
Development", STEM and "physical education and health", while your vote goes to
the independent "Sarah Smith" for art and creativity. Because you feel that your
views on "art and creativity" align more closely to "Sarah Smith" than to the
"party line" of "political party" A.

So on and so forth, all the way down to the finest grained issues, which are
debated in "chat rooms" and settled by a vote. Each "politically active" citizen
gets one vote in the "chat room" per how many votes from "regular citizens" have
been delegated to them.

"politically active" citizens and "political parties" can "campaign" on some
public forums (like facebook) trying to convince "regular citizens" to delegate
their vote to them.

## One transcription note

The worked example says *"Health decomposes as Early Childhood Development,
STEM, art and creativity, physical education and health"* and then delegates
"A to Education ... refine your vote to Education". Those four are Education's
sub-issues, not Health's. The structure is unambiguous either way — a category
refines into sub-categories, and a delegation at a node may be overridden at any
descendant — so the slip is recorded rather than corrected.
